## 2. Why the breakthrough wasn't real

### R9b sweep produced positives
3 candidates × 7 N × 6 friction = 126 configurations swept. Several positive at low friction. **Multiple-comparison risk acknowledged in advisor call**.

### R9c strict OOS test on α N=4 (largest sample, n=195)
Pre-registered:
1. **WF 5-fold ≥3 positive @ 0.04** → **2/5** (folds 1, 2 had no trades — early-data regime gap)
2. **3-way test split positive** → **PASS** (+0.015%)
3. **Bootstrap mean+pos_rate** → **FAIL** (mean +0.007 but pos_rate **9%** — only 9% of 3-day windows positive, power-law tail not stable distribution)
4. Friction sensitivity (0.02/0.04/0.06 all positive) → **PASS**
5. Robustness (WR 46.7, RR 1.31, n=195) → **PASS**

**Overall**: FAIL (3 of 5). Per pre-registration, **drop the claim**. Multiple-comparison sweep noise confirmed.

## 3. Final convergent finding

Across 17 unique mechanisms × 9 sequential rounds × 2 criterion levels × 1 OOS verification:

**Zero strategies pass any defensible production criterion**. The strongest C1 evidence (ι Δp50 +0.226), the cleverest exit modification (fixed N=4), and the deepest verification all fail OOS.

### Why multiplicative gap is structural
- ι entry alpha gross @ N=16 fixed exit: ~+0.017%/day
- BingX maker rebate floor: ~0.04% RT (best case, 100% maker fills)
- 0.04% × 0.45 trades/day = -0.018%/day friction
- Net @ best-case: ~-0.001%/day (essentially zero)
- User criterion +0.2%/day → **>10× gap remains under any realistic friction assumption**

### Why fixed N didn't save it
α N=4 produces positive *full-sample* daily but:
- WF folds 1-2 no trades → strategy regime-dependent
- Bootstrap 91% windows non-positive → most periods unprofitable
- Sample-period leverage on a few good months

This is exactly the "looks good in aggregate, falls apart in OOS slices" pattern that pre-registered tests are designed to catch.
